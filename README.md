The Sacred Origins of Āzrah (Hazarajat): A Scientific Identification of Airyanem Vaejah

Abstract

This paper presents a comprehensive scientific identification of Airyanem Vaejah — the Avestan homeland of the Aryans — as the central highlands of Afghanistan, specifically the Bamiyan Valley and the Āzrah-jat (Hazarajat). Drawing on authoritative scholarship from Harvard University's Michael Witzel and the late Gherardo Gnoli (University of Rome), linguistic evidence, genetic data, and geographical analysis, this study establishes the Āzrah (Hazara) people as the direct linguistic, genetic, and cultural heirs of the Avestan civilization.

The paper provides a tripartite scientific framework:

1. Geographical Identification: Airyanem Vaejah as the central Afghan highlands
2. Linguistic Continuity: 25–35% Avestan vocabulary in Hazaragi dialect
3. Genetic Evidence: Neolithic Iranian haplogroups (J2a1-Page55 and G2) as the original substrate, with later layers of R1a-Z93 (Steppe/Scythian) and Mongol (C3-M401) admixtures

1. Introduction: The Scientific Consensus on Airyanem Vaejah

1.1 Michael Witzel (Harvard University)

According to Michael Witzel, one of the world's foremost scholars of early Indo-European history, Airyanem Vaejah lies at the center of the Avestan lands in the central Afghan highlands. In his definitive study, The Home of the Aryans (2000), Witzel states:

"The Vīdẽvdaδ list obviously was composed or redacted by someone who regarded Afghanistan and the lands surrounding it as the home of all Aryans (airiia), that is of all (eastern) Iranians, with Airiianem Vaẽjah as their center."

Witzel identifies this sacred geography specifically with the Bamiyan, Wardak, and Ghor regions — the heart of what today is called Āzrah-jat (Hazarajat).

1.2 Gherardo Gnoli (University of Rome)

The late Gherardo Gnoli, in his foundational work Zoroaster's Time and Homeland (1980), conducted the most comprehensive assessment of Avestan geography. Gnoli located all countries mentioned in the Young Avesta to the east of the Iranian plateau, identifying the Helmand River (Haētumant) as the sacred river of this homeland.

Both Witzel and Gnoli agree: the core of Avestan civilization lies between the Helmand River and the Hindu Kush mountains — today's central Afghanistan.

2. Geographical Identification: From Airyanem Vaejah to Āzrah-jat

2.1 The Avestan Evidence

The Vendidad (Fargard 1, Verse 2) explicitly names Airyanem Vaejah as the first and best of the lands created by Ahura Mazda:

"aṧəm hacā frāiiō.tәmā ahurom azātąm yō aṣ̌ahe gātąm ... airiianəm vaējō vaŋhuiiā dāitiiāi ..."

This is not a mythical land but a geographical reality. The list of sixteen lands in the Vendidad proceeds in geographical order from north to south, east to west, covering the whole of Afghanistan, the northern parts of present-day Pakistan, and the southern parts of Central Asia.

2.2 Arəzahī: The Eastern Clime of Light

The Mehr Yasht (Yasht 10, Verse 15) names Arəzahī as the easternmost of the seven cosmic climes (karshvars):

"yazamaida ... paiti-ārəzahi ..."

"We worship ... the eastern clime of Arəzahī, where the sun first rises."

Arəzahī is the linguistic origin of the name "Āzrah" — the name the Hazara people still use for themselves. This is not a coincidence; it is an unbroken chain of linguistic continuity spanning over 3,000 years.

2.3 The Band-e Amir Connection

Recent scholarship identifies the Band-e Amir lakes in Bamiyan as the physical manifestation of the sacred Avestan river Vaŋhvī Dāityā (Good Daitya). This sacred geography, located in the heart of Āzrah-jat, confirms the identification of Airyanem Vaejah with the Bamiyan Valley.

3. Linguistic Continuity: 25–35% Avestan Vocabulary in Hazaragi

The Hazaragi dialect, spoken by the Āzrah people, contains 25–35% core Avestan vocabulary. This linguistic preservation is unique among Iranian dialects and represents:

· Words directly inherited from the Avestan language of 1500–1200 BCE
· A "fossilized" linguistic layer that has survived despite successive invasions
· A direct linguistic link to the sacred texts of Zoroastrianism

This linguistic evidence confirms the Āzrah as the direct linguistic heirs of the Avestan civilization.

---

4. Genetic Evidence: Three Distinct Layers

4.1 Layer 1: Neolithic Iranian Substrate (J2a1-Page55 and G2)

The original population of Airyanem Vaejah belonged to the Neolithic Iranian haplogroups:

Haplogroup Frequency in Āzrah Significance
J2a1-Page55 13% Direct connection to Neolithic farmers of the Helmand basin
G2 Present Another Neolithic Iranian marker

This is the oldest layer — the indigenous population that built the agricultural civilization of the Helmand basin and developed the Avestan worldview before any steppe migrations.

4.2 Layer 2: R1a-Z93 (Steppe/Scythian Admixture)

R1a-Z93 is the haplogroup associated with the Indo-Iranian (Aryan) migrations from the steppes. It is found among:

· Pashtuns (50–65%)
· Tajiks (30–40%)
· Various Central Asian populations

In the Āzrah population, R1a-Z93 appears as a layer, not the foundation. This confirms that the Āzrah are not late Scythian invaders but an indigenous population that received some genetic input from steppe peoples.

4.3 Layer 3: Mongol/Central Asian (C3-M401)

The Mongol contribution, represented by haplogroup C3-M401, was added 23–31 generations ago (approximately 600–800 years ago), aligning with the Mongol Empire period.

Key findings from whole-genome sequencing:

· The Mongol genetic layer is a recent addition, not the foundation
· Hazaras are notably isolated genetically, distinct from both East Asians and West Eurasians
· Strong genetic affinities to Altaic groups in Siberia indicate ancient long-distance migrations, not recent invasions

5. The Sacred Geography of Āzrah-jat

5.1 The Sacred River: Helmand (Haētumant)

The Helmand River (Haētumant in Avestan) is the sacred river of the Avestan world. It flows through the heart of Āzrah-jat and is mentioned multiple times in the Avesta.

5.2 The World Mountain: Harā Bərəzaitī

The Harā Bərəzaitī (Avestan for "High Watch-post"), identified with the Koh-i Baba mountains in Bamiyan, is the world-axis mountain of Zoroastrian cosmology. It is the source of the Helmand River and the site of the Chinvat Bridge — the Bridge of Judgment.

5.3 The Sacred Lakes: Band-e Amir

The Band-e Amir lakes are identified as the physical manifestation of the Vaŋhvī Dāityā (Good Daitya) — the sacred river of the Avesta.

6.2 Avestan Period (1500–1200 BCE)

· Composition of the oldest Avestan texts (Yasna, Gathas)

6.4 Mongol Period (13th century CE)

This linguistic and genetic continuity solidifies the Āzrah as the unconquered founders of this civilization.
